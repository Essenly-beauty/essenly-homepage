You found the perfect salon on Instagram. The stylist's portfolio is incredible. But there's one problem: everything is in Korean, and they don't reply to English DMs.

Here's how to actually get an appointment.

## The Reality of Salon Booking in Seoul

Let's be honest about the current state of things:

- **Instagram DMs** — Most popular salons get hundreds of DMs. English ones often get skipped because staff can't read them.
- **Klook/Trazy packages** — Convenient but expensive. You'll pay 2-3x the local price and have limited stylist choices.
- **Walk-ins** — Possible at chain salons, but popular independent salons are booked days in advance.
- **Naver Booking** — The local standard, but entirely in Korean.

## Option 1: The DIY Approach

If you want to book yourself, here's the step-by-step:

### Step 1: Find the Salon's Naver Page

Search the salon name in Korean on Naver. Most salons have a Naver Place listing with:
- Photos and reviews
- Menu with prices
- A booking button (예약)

### Step 2: Use Papago to Translate

Naver's Papago translator is more accurate than Google Translate for Korean. Use the camera feature to translate menus in real-time.

### Step 3: Prepare Key Phrases

Save these on your phone:

- **예약하고 싶습니다** (yeyak-hago sipseumnida) — I'd like to make a reservation
- **컷 해주세요** (keot haejuseyo) — A haircut, please
- **염색 해주세요** (yeomsaek haejuseyo) — Hair coloring, please
- **펌 해주세요** (peom haejuseyo) — A perm, please
- **트리트먼트 해주세요** (teuriteumenteu haejuseyo) — A treatment, please

### Step 4: Bring Reference Photos

This is the most important tip. Korean stylists are visual — bring 3-5 photos of exactly what you want. Save them on your phone, don't just bookmark them.

## Option 2: Ask Your Hotel/Hostel

Hotel concierges in tourist areas usually have salon connections and can call to book for you. Some higher-end hotels have partnerships with nearby salons.

## What to Expect at a Korean Salon

### Pricing
- Women's cut: 20,000 - 50,000 won ($15-38)
- Men's cut: 15,000 - 30,000 won ($11-23)
- Color: 60,000 - 150,000 won ($45-115)
- Perm: 80,000 - 200,000 won ($60-150)
- Treatment: 30,000 - 80,000 won ($23-60)

These are *local* prices. If a service targeting tourists costs more than this, you're overpaying.

### Tipping
There is **no tipping culture** in Korean salons. The price is the price.

### The Experience
Korean salons typically include a complimentary shampoo and blow-dry with any service. Many also offer tea or coffee while you wait. The attention to detail is something else — don't be surprised if your stylist spends 30 minutes on your blow-dry alone.

## Pro Tips

1. **Book for weekday mornings** — Less crowded, more attention from the stylist
2. **Screenshot the Korean address** — You'll need it for the taxi. Use the Essenly app's "Show to taxi driver" feature
3. **Ask for the designer, not the intern** — Request "디자이너" (dijaneo) specifically when booking
4. **Cash is sometimes cheaper** — Some salons offer a card-free discount
